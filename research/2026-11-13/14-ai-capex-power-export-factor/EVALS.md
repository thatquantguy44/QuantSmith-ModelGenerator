# EVALS — AI Infrastructure Capex-to-Power-and-Export Supply Chain Factor
**Status:** Design for tests, no experiment performed.

## Economic claim and comparator
- **H1:** Public capex-to-power-to-export bottleneck signals predict medium-horizon revisions across semiconductor equipment, utilities and data-center supply chains.
- **Comparator:** Equal-weight US tech/utility/semiconductor thematic basket with broad market hedge..
- **Candidate:** Supply-chain bottleneck score anchored in publicly observed physical constraints..
- **Advanced alternative:** Bayesian uncertainty propagation across disclosed commitments, supply chains and commodity/electricity capacity..
- **Metrics:** sector-neutral residual return, realized earnings revision, stock/industry turnover and capacity sensitivity..
- **Stop/no-go:** Reject if holdout earnings-revision association and after-cost portfolio uplift vanish after mega-cap momentum controls.
- **Cost sensitivity:** USD $180k hypothetical 24-month engineering/data/maintenance cost; gross benefit sensitivity $0k–$560k over 24 months. Net ROI = (gross benefit − cost)/cost = **-100% to 211%**. NOT forecast or historical measurement. Economic lever: hypothetical $15m thematic sleeve; 0–185 bps annualized incremental after-cost return within favorable scenario; zero benefit base case.

## Evaluation splits and safeguards
1. Set chronologically non-overlapping training, validation and holdout periods; embargo trades with outcome windows crossing splits.
2. Align all records to first permitted information availability, not economic reference date. rebalance monthly after latest public filings, energy updates and international trade release
3. Use exactly matched feasible opportunity sets and complete funding, execution, regulatory and operational frictions for baseline/candidate.
4. Compare against **no-action** when the cost of the position or data is not measurable.
5. Use time-blocked paired bootstrap uncertainty, relevant alternative market regimes and explicit multiple-hypothesis correction.
6. Test adverse implementation constraints: Cap exposure to liquid industry ETFs/stocks; forecast vintages and reasonable turnover restrictions.
7. Stress identification risk: Disclosure boilerplate, future guidance and partial energy-grid footprint can generate strong hindsight mapping bias.
8. Record both gross and net metrics; a statistically significant signal is not necessarily profitable or operationally feasible.
