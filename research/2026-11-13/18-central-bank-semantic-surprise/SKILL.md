# SKILL — Central-Bank Communication Semantic Surprise Across Languages
## Trigger
Use this skill when researching: **Quantify official policy-message *differences* between successive statements to adjust next-session FX/rates exposure.** in **US Federal Reserve, ECB and selected Asian monetary authorities**.

## Inputs
Source/version references and license, decision clock, central-bank event × original language × statement version × publication timestamp, the cost model, allowed actions, and study windows. Minimum fields: Versioned official policy statements; actual publication timestamps; pre-event interest rate futures; FX and rates price bars; translated text audit..

## Procedure
1. Load SPEC and HANDOFF; state hypothesis and category B · Domestic & global markets.
2. Build source-claim ledger. Do not conflate official data description and available executable market dataset.
3. Check first public availability and carry correction vintages forward, never backward.
4. Create adverse synthetic fixtures and reject invalid market constraints.
5. Fit and run **Policy rate surprise (or no-surprise dummy without licensed expectation) and prior-day rate changes.** on training data with full costs.
6. Define **Source-grounded claim-difference extractor with multilingual uncertainty and simple linear evaluation.**, plus optional **Constrained multilingual textual event state-space with calibration and abstention threshold.** conditional on simple model advantage.
7. Freeze chronological holdouts and evaluate **out-of-sample price direction log loss, FX hedge information ratio, translation agreement and unsupported claim rate.** plus capacity and tail risk.
8. Try to falsify: **Reject if no incremental holdout log-loss improvement or semantic extractor makes unsupported claims on challenge set.**.
9. Record all negative tests, unverified sources, measured trade-offs and manual approval gate.
10. Return BLOCKED if Translation distortions, backfilled statement updates, and post-event news leakage are especially severe for multilingual event models. cannot be mitigated.
