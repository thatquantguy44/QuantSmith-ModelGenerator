# Research workflow — Cross-Border Settlement Calendar Bridge Planner

1. **Data => rights/time audit.** Ingest obligation leg x settlement service x UTC instant through proposed `settlement_calendars`; save exact release bytes privately where rights permit and only public-safe manifests here. Check Plan at least two local business days before a known holiday, using rules announced by then. Unexpected outages are separate stress scenarios; announced future changes enter only after their publication. Unknown data rights or availability yields BLOCKED.
2. **Hypothesis => pre-registration.** Freeze A service-specific dated-calendar planner eliminates at least 90% of calendar-caused exceptions and lowers bridge funding cost by 10% versus a fixed two-business-day prefunding rule. Define native-horizon targets and independent event groups; lock costs, limits and partition dates before seeing holdout outcomes.
3. **Baseline => reproducible control.** Implement Prefund every cross-border obligation two generic business days early, with manual holiday review. Verify Replay US/Europe DST mismatch, Good Friday and a service-specific cutoff extension; the planner must distinguish securities and cash service availability. Keep equal information and economic constraints across methods.
4. **Candidate => interpretable action.** Build service open intervals, version effective dates, cash-day gaps and DST conversion; fit/solve Time-expanded multi-service cash-flow network. Return prefunding dates, minimal cash bridge and infeasible-leg alerts plus a feasibility/time certificate. Advanced research `Robust network flow with uncertain service extensions and intraday cash arrival bands` stays optional.
5. **Validation => objective verdict.** Report calendar exceptions, cash-days, funding expense, minimum liquidity and cutoff breaches. GO if >=90% calendar exceptions avoided and >=10% net bridge-cost saving on >=24 holiday windows; zero infeasible settlement recommendations. STOP if routing assumptions require unapproved service access. Double execution costs and halve capacity; include zero-benefit and delayed-launch ROI scenarios.
6. **Review => private shadow.** Domain reviewer challenges Future operating-day changes differ between funds and securities services. DST, half-days and local date conventions can introduce 24-hour errors; a public calendar is not a service entitlement. Complete rights and capacity evidence before any prospective shadow run. Human review is required; runtime deployment and trading need separate authority.

All transitions are future gates, currently BLOCKED. A failed experiment is retained with source/config/code hashes; reruns cannot overwrite an adverse outcome or silently relax a threshold. Generated reports label FACT/HYPOTHESIS/ASSUMPTION/CALCULATION/UNVERIFIED and preserve the original baseline alongside candidate output.
